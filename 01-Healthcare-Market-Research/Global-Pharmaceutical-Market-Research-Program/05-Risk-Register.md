# Risk Register

## 1. Purpose

The Risk Register identifies, assesses, and monitors potential events that could negatively affect the successful delivery of the Global Pharmaceutical Market Research Program.

Risks are evaluated based on their probability and potential impact on project scope, schedule, quality, data integrity, stakeholder expectations, and final deliverables.

---

## 2. Risk Scoring Methodology

### Probability

| Score | Rating    | Definition             |
| ----: | --------- | ---------------------- |
|     1 | Very Low  | Unlikely to occur      |
|     2 | Low       | Relatively unlikely    |
|     3 | Medium    | Possible               |
|     4 | High      | Likely to occur        |
|     5 | Very High | Highly likely to occur |

### Impact

| Score | Rating    | Definition         |
| ----: | --------- | ------------------ |
|     1 | Very Low  | Minimal effect     |
|     2 | Low       | Limited effect     |
|     3 | Medium    | Moderate effect    |
|     4 | High      | Significant effect |
|     5 | Very High | Severe effect      |

### Risk Score

**Risk Score = Probability × Impact**

| Score Range | Risk Level |
| ----------: | ---------- |
|         1–4 | Low        |
|         5–9 | Moderate   |
|       10–15 | High       |
|       16–25 | Critical   |

## 4. High-Priority Risks

### R01 — Data Quality

**Risk:** Survey data may contain missing, inconsistent, or unexpected values.

**Potential impact:**
Poor data quality could affect analytical calculations, KPI accuracy, dashboard outputs, and business conclusions.

**Response:** Mitigate

**Action:** Perform data profiling and quality checks before beginning quantitative analysis.

---

### R02 — Requirements Uncertainty

**Risk:** Business or research requirements may be unclear or change during execution.

**Potential impact:**
Requirement changes can result in rework, scope expansion, and schedule delays.

**Response:** Mitigate

**Action:** Establish clear requirements and validate them before analytical development begins.

---

### R05 — KPI Definition Ambiguity

**Risk:** Different stakeholders may interpret KPIs differently.

**Potential impact:**
Incorrect interpretation could reduce confidence in the dashboard and lead to inconsistent reporting.

**Response:** Mitigate

**Action:** Define KPI calculations, business meaning, and measurement logic before dashboard development.

---

### R06 — Analytical Errors

**Risk:** Errors in calculations or data transformations could produce inaccurate findings.

**Potential impact:**
Incorrect results could affect business interpretation and stakeholder confidence.

**Response:** Mitigate

**Action:** Validate calculations against source data and conduct structured quality checks.

---

### R10 — Misinterpretation of Findings

**Risk:** Portfolio research findings could be interpreted as clinical or regulatory evidence.

**Potential impact:**
This could create inappropriate conclusions about pharmaceutical products or healthcare outcomes.

**Response:** Avoid

**Action:** Clearly communicate the analytical and illustrative nature of the project and document its limitations.

---

## 5. Risk Response Strategies

The project uses four primary response strategies:

### Avoid

Change the project approach to eliminate the risk.

Example:

Avoiding any presentation of portfolio findings as clinical or regulatory evidence.

### Mitigate

Reduce the probability or impact of the risk.

Example:

Performing early data-quality checks.

### Transfer

Shift responsibility for managing a risk to another party where appropriate.

This strategy is not expected to be heavily used in this portfolio project.

### Accept

Acknowledge the risk and proceed without additional preventive action when the potential impact is low or the cost of mitigation exceeds the expected benefit.

---

## 6. Risk Monitoring Approach

Risks should be reviewed throughout the project lifecycle.

The Project Manager should:

* Review high and critical risks regularly.
* Update probability and impact when conditions change.
* Track mitigation actions.
* Identify newly emerging risks.
* Escalate risks requiring stakeholder decisions.
* Monitor risks associated with scope and schedule changes.
* Close risks when they are no longer applicable.

---

## 7. Risk Escalation

Risks should be escalated when:

* The risk becomes critical.
* Mitigation actions are unsuccessful.
* The risk threatens a major milestone.
* The risk could materially affect project scope.
* Data quality compromises analytical validity.
* Stakeholder disagreement prevents progress.
* A risk requires a decision outside the Project Manager's authority.

---

## 9. Risk Status Categories

Risks may be tracked using the following statuses:

* **Open** — Risk requires monitoring or action.
* **Mitigating** — Mitigation activity is underway.
* **Escalated** — Risk requires higher-level attention.
* **Accepted** — Risk has been consciously accepted.
* **Closed** — Risk is no longer applicable.

---

## 10. Risk Management Success Criteria

Risk management will be considered effective when:

1. Major project risks are identified early.
2. Risks are assessed consistently.
3. High and critical risks have documented mitigation actions.
4. Risk owners understand their responsibilities.
5. Emerging risks are captured during project execution.
6. Risk responses are monitored.
7. Risks are considered during project decisions and change management.
8. No major project deliverable is completed without appropriate risk review.
