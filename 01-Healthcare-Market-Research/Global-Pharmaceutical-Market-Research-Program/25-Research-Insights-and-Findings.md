# Research Insights & Findings

## 1. Purpose

This document translates the physician survey analysis and Power BI dashboard outputs into structured business insights.

The objective is to move beyond descriptive reporting and identify meaningful implications for pharmaceutical market research, adoption strategy, stakeholder engagement, and implementation planning.

The findings are based on the available physician survey dataset and the analytical measures documented in the KPI & DAX Documentation.

---

# 2. Executive Summary

The analysis of **500 physician respondents** indicates a strong overall willingness to adopt the relevant pharmaceutical or healthcare solutions.

The **Average Adoption Likelihood is 4.23 out of 5**, representing the strongest headline signal in the analysis.

However, the adoption opportunity exists alongside several potential barriers. **Administrative complexity** is identified as the leading adoption barrier, followed by cost/access, physician familiarity, limited evidence, patient acceptance, and workflow impact.

Perceived effectiveness is more moderate, with an overall **Average Effectiveness of 3.42 out of 5**.

The overall **Average Administrative Burden is 3.03 out of 5**, indicating that administrative considerations remain meaningful even in the presence of strong adoption intent.

Treatment-level safety ratings shown in the dashboard remain relatively stable in the mid-3 range, with the highest displayed treatment-level rating at approximately **3.7/5**.

Overall, the findings suggest a potentially favorable adoption environment, but successful adoption may depend on reducing operational friction, improving accessibility, strengthening physician familiarity, and addressing evidence and workflow concerns.

# 3. Key Finding 1 — Strong Adoption Potential

## Evidence

The dashboard reports:

**Average Adoption Likelihood = 4.23 / 5**

Adoption likelihood is also relatively consistent across the physician specialties displayed in the dashboard:

| Specialty     | Adoption Likelihood |
| ------------- | ------------------: |
| Cardiology    |                 4.3 |
| Neurology     |                 4.3 |
| Immunology    |                 4.2 |
| Endocrinology |                 4.2 |
| Oncology      |                 4.2 |

## Interpretation

The consistency across the displayed specialties suggests that strong adoption intent is not concentrated in only one specialty.

The difference between the highest and lowest displayed specialty averages is approximately **0.1 points**, indicating a relatively narrow range.

## Business Implication

The findings suggest that the potential adoption opportunity may extend across multiple physician segments rather than being restricted to a single specialty.

This could support broader stakeholder discussions around:

* Market positioning
* Physician engagement
* Specialty-specific messaging
* Adoption planning
* Further segmentation analysis

## Caveat

High stated adoption likelihood should not be interpreted as a forecast of actual prescribing or market adoption.

Actual adoption may be influenced by factors such as reimbursement, access, workflow, evidence, patient acceptance, and implementation complexity.

# 5. Key Finding 3 — Effectiveness Perception Is Moderate

## Evidence

The dashboard reports:

**Average Effectiveness = 3.42 / 5**

Treatment-level effectiveness values displayed in the dashboard are:

| Treatment   | Effectiveness Rating |
| ----------- | -------------------: |
| Treatment A |                  3.6 |
| Treatment B |                  3.4 |
| Treatment D |                  3.4 |
| Treatment C |                  3.3 |

## Interpretation

Perceived effectiveness is positive but materially lower than the overall adoption likelihood score.

The difference between the overall adoption score and effectiveness score is approximately:

**4.23 − 3.42 = 0.81 points**

This indicates that strong adoption intent cannot be explained solely by very high perceived effectiveness.

## Business Implication

Further research may be useful to understand which other factors contribute to adoption intent.

Potential areas include:

* Physician familiarity
* Patient acceptance
* Safety perception
* Access
* Evidence availability
* Workflow considerations
* Administrative burden

# 6. Key Finding 4 — Administrative Burden Remains Meaningful

## Evidence

The dashboard reports:

**Average Administrative Burden = 3.03 / 5**

Administrative complexity is also identified as the leading primary adoption barrier.

## Interpretation

The combination of a moderate administrative-burden score and administrative complexity ranking as the leading barrier suggests that operational friction deserves attention.

The finding is particularly relevant because adoption likelihood remains high.

## Business Implication

There may be an opportunity to improve adoption by addressing the operational experience surrounding the solution rather than focusing exclusively on physician interest.

Potential interventions could include:

* Simplified administrative workflows
* Clear implementation guidance
* Better support materials
* Workflow integration
* Process redesign
* Training and education

These should be treated as potential areas for further research rather than confirmed causal solutions.

# 8. Key Finding 6 — Adoption Opportunity and Operational Friction Coexist

One of the most important observations from the dashboard is the combination of:

| Metric                        |       Result |
| ----------------------------- | -----------: |
| Average Adoption Likelihood   | **4.23 / 5** |
| Average Effectiveness         | **3.42 / 5** |
| Average Administrative Burden | **3.03 / 5** |

## Interpretation

Physicians appear highly willing to adopt the relevant solutions, while effectiveness perception is more moderate and administrative burden remains a meaningful concern.

This creates a potentially important business question:

> **What is driving strong adoption intent despite moderate effectiveness perception and operational barriers?**

## Recommended Further Analysis

A deeper analysis could investigate relationships between:

* Adoption likelihood and effectiveness
* Adoption likelihood and administrative burden
* Adoption likelihood and safety
* Adoption likelihood and physician specialty
* Adoption likelihood and physician familiarity
* Adoption likelihood and access/cost considerations

This would allow stakeholders to move from descriptive findings toward a more structured understanding of adoption drivers.

# 14. Analytical Limitations

The findings should be interpreted within the limitations of the available dataset.

### Descriptive Analysis

The dashboard primarily presents descriptive statistics and comparisons.

### No Causal Inference

The findings do not establish that any particular factor causes adoption.

### Survey Intent vs. Actual Behavior

Stated adoption likelihood does not necessarily predict actual prescribing or adoption behavior.

### Dataset Scope

The analysis is limited to the available physician survey variables.

### No Clinical or Regulatory Conclusions

The findings should not be interpreted as clinical evidence, regulatory evidence, or medical recommendations.

### Further Validation Required

Additional primary research, statistical analysis, and real-world evidence would be required to validate broader commercial conclusions.

# 15. Executive Takeaway

The physician survey analysis presents a potentially favorable adoption environment, with an overall adoption likelihood of **4.23/5 across 500 respondents**.

However, the analysis also identifies an important execution challenge: **administrative complexity is the leading adoption barrier**.

Perceived effectiveness is more moderate at **3.42/5**, while administrative burden averages **3.03/5**.

The combination suggests that future market-research and implementation planning should not focus solely on physician willingness to adopt. Stakeholders should also investigate the operational, access, evidence, familiarity, and patient-related factors that may influence successful adoption.
