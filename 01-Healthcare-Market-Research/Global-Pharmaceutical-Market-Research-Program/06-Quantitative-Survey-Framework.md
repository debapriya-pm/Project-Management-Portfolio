# Quantitative Survey Framework

## Study

Physician Treatment Decision-Making and Unmet Needs Study

## Parent Program

Global Pharmaceutical Market Research Program

## Method

Quantitative Physician Survey

## Client

NovaCura Pharmaceuticals (Fictional)

---

# 1. Research Purpose

The quantitative survey is designed to validate and measure themes identified during the qualitative research phase.

The survey will provide measurable evidence regarding physician treatment decisions, unmet needs, treatment preferences, competitive perceptions, and barriers to adoption.

---

# 2. Research Objectives

The quantitative study aims to:

1. Measure the importance of treatment decision drivers.
2. Quantify physician perceptions of unmet needs.
3. Assess awareness and perceptions of treatment options.
4. Measure barriers to treatment adoption.
5. Identify differences between physician segments.
6. Quantify potential opportunities identified during qualitative research.
7. Provide evidence to support commercial decision-making.

---

# 3. Target Respondents

The target respondent population will consist of qualified physicians and specialists with relevant experience in the selected therapeutic area.

### Illustrative qualification criteria

Respondents should:

- Be licensed healthcare professionals.
- Currently practice in the relevant therapeutic area.
- Have recent experience treating relevant patients.
- Meet minimum patient-volume criteria where applicable.
- Be able to provide informed professional responses.

Final inclusion and exclusion criteria would be confirmed during study design.

---

# 4. Indicative Sample Framework

The final sample size would be determined based on the research objectives, statistical requirements, market coverage, and available research budget.

Illustrative framework:

| Segment | Example |
|---|---|
| Geography | Multiple target markets |
| Specialty | Relevant physician specialties |
| Experience | Different experience levels |
| Practice Setting | Hospital / Private / Academic |
| Patient Volume | Low / Medium / High |

Where appropriate, quotas may be established to ensure sufficient representation of priority segments.

# 6. Section A — Screening

The screening section will confirm respondent eligibility.

### Example screening areas

- Professional role
- Specialty
- Current practice
- Relevant patient experience
- Treatment involvement
- Geographic eligibility

Respondents who do not meet the predefined criteria will not proceed to the main questionnaire.

---

# 7. Section B — Physician Profile

The questionnaire may capture:

- Specialty
- Years of experience
- Practice setting
- Geographic market
- Approximate patient volume
- Relevant treatment experience

These variables can support segmentation and subgroup analysis.

---

# 8. Section C — Treatment Landscape

The survey will measure physician perspectives regarding the current treatment environment.

### Example measures

- Treatments currently used
- Frequency of use
- Treatment sequencing
- Perceived effectiveness
- Patient suitability
- Overall satisfaction

---

# 9. Section D — Treatment Decision Drivers

The survey will quantify the importance of factors identified during qualitative research.

### Example attributes

Respondents may be asked to rate the importance of:

- Clinical effectiveness
- Safety
- Tolerability
- Patient outcomes
- Ease of administration
- Convenience
- Evidence base
- Patient preference
- Access
- Cost
- Administrative burden

### Example Question

**How important is each of the following when selecting a treatment option?**

Response scale:

1 — Not at all important  
2 — Slightly important  
3 — Moderately important  
4 — Very important  
5 — Extremely important

---

# 10. Section E — Unmet Needs

The survey will quantify the unmet needs identified during qualitative research.

### Example Question

**Which areas represent the greatest unmet needs in the current treatment pathway?**

Potential response options:

- Treatment effectiveness
- Side-effect management
- Patient adherence
- Treatment convenience
- Administration burden
- Access
- Monitoring
- Patient support
- Other

Respondents may be asked to rank or rate the importance of selected needs.

---

# 11. Section F — Competitive Perceptions

The study may evaluate perceptions of current treatment options.

Potential attributes include:

- Effectiveness
- Safety
- Convenience
- Ease of use
- Patient suitability
- Evidence
- Overall value
- Differentiation

Where appropriate, respondents may rate or compare selected treatment options.

---

# 12. Section G — Adoption Barriers

The questionnaire will measure factors that may prevent or delay adoption of newer treatments.

### Example barriers

- Lack of clinical evidence
- Limited physician familiarity
- Patient acceptance
- Access restrictions
- Cost
- Administrative complexity
- Workflow disruption
- Availability
- Lack of experience

### Example Question

**How significant is each factor as a barrier to adopting a new treatment option?**

Response scale:

1 — Not a barrier  
2 — Minor barrier  
3 — Moderate barrier  
4 — Significant barrier  
5 — Major barrier

---

# 13. Section H — Concept Evaluation

A hypothetical treatment concept may be introduced based on the study objectives.

Respondents may evaluate:

- Overall appeal
- Relevance
- Perceived differentiation
- Likelihood of consideration
- Potential patient suitability
- Concerns
- Information requirements

### Example Question

Response scale:

1 — Very unlikely  
2 — Unlikely  
3 — Neutral  
4 — Likely  
5 — Very likely

---

# 16. Qualitative-to-Quantitative Link

The quantitative questionnaire will be informed by themes identified during the qualitative phase.

### Example

**Qualitative finding:**

Physicians describe administrative complexity as a barrier to treatment adoption.

↓

**Quantitative validation:**

Measure:

- Percentage of physicians identifying administrative complexity as a barrier.
- Relative importance compared with other barriers.
- Differences across physician segments.
- Differences across markets.

↓

**Business insight:**

Determine whether administrative complexity represents a broad market opportunity or a segment-specific issue.
