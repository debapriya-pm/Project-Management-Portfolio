# Lessons Learned

## 1. Purpose

The Lessons Learned document captures key observations, challenges, successful practices, and improvement opportunities identified during the Global Pharmaceutical Market Research Program.

The objective is to support continuous improvement and demonstrate how project experience can be translated into better practices for future healthcare market-research and data-driven projects.

---

## 2. What Worked Well

### 2.1 Clear Project Structure

Establishing the project framework before moving into detailed analysis helped create a logical connection between business objectives, requirements, data, analysis, and reporting.

The sequence:

**Charter → Scope → WBS → Schedule → Risks → RACI → Data → Analysis → Dashboard**

provided a structured delivery approach.

---

### 2.2 Early Definition of Business Questions

Defining the business questions before developing the dashboard helped ensure that the analytical work remained connected to business needs.

The core questions focused on:

* Adoption
* Effectiveness
* Administrative burden
* Safety
* Adoption barriers
* Unmet needs

This reduced the risk of creating visualizations without a clear business purpose.

---

### 2.3 Data Quality Considerations

Performing a data-quality assessment before relying on the analytical results highlighted the importance of:

* Completeness
* Validity
* Consistency
* Uniqueness
* Analytical fitness

This reinforced the principle that dashboard development should follow data validation rather than precede it.

---

### 2.4 KPI Documentation

Documenting the KPI definitions and DAX measures improved transparency between the underlying dataset and dashboard outputs.

The KPI documentation established traceability from:

**Business Requirement → Source Field → DAX Measure → Dashboard KPI**

This makes analytical outputs easier for stakeholders to understand and validate.

---

### 2.5 Executive-Focused Reporting

The Power BI dashboard was designed to present key findings through:

* KPI cards
* Specialty-level adoption analysis
* Adoption barriers
* Unmet needs
* Treatment effectiveness
* Treatment safety

This helped transform raw survey data into a management-oriented reporting format.

---

# 3. Challenges Identified

## 3.1 Data Context Limitations

The available dataset provides useful physician-level survey information but does not necessarily contain every variable required to explain adoption behavior.

Additional information may be needed to investigate:

* Market access
* Reimbursement
* Prescribing behavior
* Patient outcomes
* Longitudinal adoption
* Clinical evidence
* Geographic differences

### Lesson

A strong analytical result is dependent not only on data quality but also on whether the dataset contains the variables needed to answer the business question.

---

## 3.2 Distinguishing Correlation From Causation

The dashboard identifies patterns between adoption, effectiveness, administrative burden, safety, and other factors.

However, descriptive analysis does not establish causation.

### Lesson

Business stakeholders should be provided with clear interpretation guidelines so that descriptive findings are not presented as causal conclusions.

---

## 3.3 Balancing Technical and Business Communication

DAX measures and data-model concepts may be useful to analytics stakeholders but can be unnecessary for executive audiences.

### Lesson

Different stakeholder groups require different levels of detail.

Technical documentation should support transparency, while executive reporting should focus on:

* What happened?
* Why does it matter?
* What should we investigate next?

---

## 3.4 Dashboard Scope Management

A market-research dataset can support many possible analyses.

Without clear scope control, dashboard development can expand indefinitely.

### Lesson

Dashboard requirements should be prioritized according to business value rather than attempting to visualize every available variable.

---

# 4. Key Project Management Lessons

### Lesson 1 — Define the Problem Before Building the Solution

The project reinforced the importance of establishing the business objective before beginning technical development.

### Lesson 2 — Requirements Drive Analytics

The dashboard should answer defined business questions rather than simply display available data.

### Lesson 3 — Data Quality Is a Project Concern

Data quality should be treated as part of project planning and governance, not only as an analytics task.

### Lesson 4 — Ownership Must Be Clear

The RACI framework helps reduce ambiguity by defining who is responsible, accountable, consulted, and informed.

### Lesson 5 — Risks Should Be Identified Early

Potential data, scope, stakeholder, schedule, and analytical risks should be considered before they become delivery problems.

### Lesson 6 — Insights Matter More Than Visuals

A visually attractive dashboard has limited business value unless stakeholders can understand what the findings mean and what decisions they may inform.

### Lesson 7 — Executive Communication Requires Prioritization

Senior stakeholders generally need the most important findings and implications rather than every underlying analytical detail.

# 5. What Could Be Improved in a Future Iteration

A future version of the project could include:

### 5.1 Additional Primary Research

Conduct qualitative interviews or structured physician discussions to explain the reasons behind quantitative findings.

### 5.2 Deeper Statistical Analysis

Apply appropriate statistical techniques to investigate relationships between adoption and other variables.

### 5.3 More Detailed Segmentation

Analyze adoption and barriers by:

* Specialty
* Experience
* Geography
* Physician characteristics
* Other available segments

### 5.4 Additional Market Variables

Incorporate information related to:

* Market access
* Reimbursement
* Pricing
* Evidence
* Competitive landscape
* Treatment pathways

### 5.5 Longitudinal Analysis

If data were available over time, analyze changes in physician perceptions and adoption intent.

### 5.6 Advanced Power BI Features

Future dashboard versions could incorporate:

* Drill-through pages
* Advanced segmentation
* Tooltips
* Bookmarks
* Additional interactive views
* More sophisticated analytical measures

---

# 6. Process Improvements for Future Projects

The following improvements would strengthen future project execution:

1. Establish the analytical data dictionary earlier.
2. Define KPI calculation rules during requirements gathering.
3. Validate source data before dashboard design.
4. Establish dashboard acceptance criteria before development.
5. Conduct stakeholder reviews at defined checkpoints.
6. Maintain a formal change log for scope changes.
7. Capture lessons learned throughout execution rather than only at closure.
8. Maintain clear traceability between requirements and deliverables.

---

# 7. Lessons for Future Healthcare Projects

Healthcare and pharmaceutical projects often involve multiple stakeholder groups with different priorities.

Future projects should therefore emphasize:

* Clear stakeholder communication
* Strong documentation
* Data governance
* Requirement traceability
* Risk management
* Regulatory awareness
* Appropriate interpretation of research findings
* Patient and physician perspectives
* Executive-level communication

# 9. Final Takeaway

The primary lesson from the project is:

> **Data becomes valuable when it is connected to a clearly defined business question, governed through a structured project approach, and translated into actionable insight.**

This principle should guide future healthcare market-research, business-analysis, and data-driven project initiatives.
