# Interview Topic Answer Bank – Tech Mahindra Program Manager, SPO

This companion bank expands the 30 scenario questions with the concepts and probes listed in sections 1–17 of the supplied guide. Adapt every personal example and number to verified experience.

## 1. Tell me about your overall project-management experience.

"I have more than nine years of professional experience across project management, PMO, healthcare operations, market research and cross-functional delivery. My work has involved coordinating multiple stakeholders, structuring project plans, tracking deliverables, managing risks and communicating progress. I hold PMP and CSM certifications. I use my healthcare and technology case studies to demonstrate how I connect business objectives, operational processes and implementation governance."

## 2. What types of projects have you handled?

"My experience spans healthcare operations, healthcare market research, cross-functional project delivery and technology-enabled workflow improvement. For this interview, I have prepared Salesforce Service Cloud as my primary implementation case study and ePA as my secondary healthcare technology example. I distinguish my actual work history from portfolio simulations and explain my personal contribution clearly."

## 3. How many projects can you manage simultaneously?

"I assess complexity, milestone criticality, stakeholder demand, resource constraints and risk rather than using a fixed number. I maintain a consolidated portfolio tracker with milestones, dependencies, owners, RAG status and decision dates. I reserve time for critical escalations, use a consistent reporting cadence and escalate capacity conflicts before they affect delivery."

## 4. Explain the project lifecycle.

"I start with initiation: business case, SOW, charter, objectives and stakeholders. In planning, I define scope, WBS, schedule, cost, resources, quality, communications and RAID. During execution, I coordinate workstreams and manage deliverables. In monitoring and controlling, I compare actuals with the baseline, manage changes, risks and issues, and report status. In closure, I secure acceptance, transfer ownership, document lessons learned and review benefits."

## 5. Which methodology do you use?

"I tailor the methodology to the delivery environment. I use Waterfall-style baselines and stage gates for scope, dependencies, approvals, cutover and handover. I use Agile practices for iterative configuration, demonstrations, backlog prioritization and defect resolution. A hybrid approach gives me governance and predictability while allowing technical teams to adapt through feedback."

## 6. How do you create a WBS?

"I start with the approved scope and deliverables, then decompose them into manageable work packages and activities. I assign each package an owner, estimate, acceptance evidence and dependency. I check that the WBS covers the full scope without duplicating work. I use it as the foundation for the schedule, resource plan and status tracking."

## 7. What is a critical path?

"The critical path is the longest chain of dependent activities that determines the earliest possible project completion. A delay to a critical-path task can delay the project finish unless I recover time or change the plan. I use MS Project to review dependencies, float and forecast dates, then focus attention on tasks with little or no schedule flexibility."

## 8. How do you manage stakeholder expectations?

"I identify stakeholder power, interest, expectations and decision authority. I agree what each group needs, how often they need updates and which forum handles decisions. I use the RACI to clarify accountability, communicate changes early and escalate unresolved conflicts through governance. I avoid promising dates or outcomes before the delivery owners validate them."

## 9. How do you influence without authority?

"I build trust through preparation, follow-through and data. I understand the stakeholder's priorities, clarify the shared objective and explain the consequence of a missed commitment. I offer options and ask for a decision rather than relying on hierarchy. If the issue exceeds my authority, I use the agreed escalation path."

## 10. What is the difference between a risk, issue, assumption and dependency?

- **Risk:** uncertain future event that may affect objectives.
- **Issue:** problem that has already occurred and requires action.
- **Assumption:** condition treated as true for planning but requiring validation.
- **Dependency:** activity, decision or deliverable that relies on another party or task.

"I record all four in the RAID log, but I use a clear type, owner, response, trigger and due date so they can be managed differently."

## 11. How do you perform root-cause analysis?

"I first define the problem and its business impact. I collect evidence such as timestamps, error logs, workflow steps and user feedback. I separate symptoms from causes using methods such as the 5 Whys or fishbone analysis. I validate the cause with the responsible team, agree corrective action, assign an owner and monitor whether the issue recurs."

## 12. What is CAPA?

"CAPA means Corrective and Preventive Action. Corrective action addresses the current problem and restores control. Preventive action changes the process, control or training so the problem is less likely to recur. I track the action owner, due date, evidence and effectiveness review."

## 13. Which BPO metrics do you understand?

"I focus on SLA adherence, service level, AHT, FCR, CSAT, quality score, occupancy, shrinkage, attrition, abandon rate and productivity. I confirm the client's definition and data source for each KPI. I also look at the relationships: for example, training can temporarily increase shrinkage, while understaffing can increase abandon rate and damage CSAT."

## 14. Explain AHT.

"Average Handling Time generally includes talk time, hold time and after-call work divided by the number of handled contacts. I use the contractual definition because some clients include or exclude particular interaction types. I monitor AHT with quality and FCR so that agents are not pressured to shorten calls at the expense of resolving the customer's issue."

## 15. Explain FCR.

"First Contact Resolution measures the percentage of eligible customer issues resolved in the first interaction, without a repeat contact for the same issue during the defined measurement window. I review repeat contacts, case reopen rates and quality findings to understand why FCR changes."

## 16. Explain occupancy and shrinkage.

"Occupancy is the share of available logged-in time spent handling contacts and related work, based on the client's definition. Shrinkage is the time agents are paid but unavailable for handling contacts, such as training, breaks, meetings and leave. WFM uses both to forecast staffing and schedule training without undermining service levels."

## 17. How do you manage resource capacity?

"I translate the schedule into role-based demand by phase. I compare required effort with available capacity, identify scarce skills and shared resources, and coordinate with functional managers. In a contact-centre transition, I work with WFM to stagger training, backfill queue hours and protect contractual SLAs."

## 18. How do you estimate and control a project budget?

"I build a bottom-up estimate using work packages, role effort, rates, licences, vendor services, infrastructure, travel if applicable, training/backfill and contingency. I baseline the budget after owners validate assumptions. I track actuals, commitments, forecast-to-complete and variance. I explain any forecast overrun and obtain approval before changing the baseline."

## 19. What is schedule variance?

"Schedule variance compares planned progress with actual progress at a point in time. In a simple milestone review, I compare baseline dates with actual or forecast dates and quantify slippage. In earned-value management, schedule variance is earned value minus planned value. I use the method agreed for the project and do not mix definitions."

## 20. How do you manage change requests?

"I log the request, confirm the rationale, assess impact on scope, cost, schedule, quality, resources, architecture, security and operations, then present options to the authorized change body. I record the decision and update the baseline only after approval. I communicate approved changes to every affected workstream."

## 21. How do you handle resistance to change?

"I involve users early, understand what is difficult about the new process and communicate why the change is happening. I use super-users, demonstrations, job aids, practice scenarios, shadowing and floor support. I track training completion, competency, adoption and user feedback, then resolve recurring usability issues with the product team."

## 22. How do you lead a cross-functional team?

"I make the outcome and responsibilities clear, break the work into owned deliverables, agree working norms and keep dependencies visible. I hold focused check-ins, remove blockers and recognize contributions. If performance or commitments slip, I discuss the issue privately, understand the cause and agree a recovery action with a due date."

## 23. How do you manage a project crisis?

"I establish a fact-based view of scope, schedule, cost, quality, risks, resources and stakeholder confidence. I identify the root causes and immediate business-continuity risks. I set up a focused recovery cadence, prioritize critical-path activities, assign owners and communicate the recovery forecast. I track actions daily until the project returns to control."

## 24. What is the purpose of a Steering Committee?

"It provides senior oversight and decision-making. It reviews business outcomes, scope, budget, schedule, top risks, cross-functional conflicts and escalations. It should focus on decisions and exceptions rather than duplicating the daily project meeting. I circulate a concise status pack and maintain a decision log."

## 25. What should a project status report contain?

"I include overall RAG, executive summary, progress against milestones, scope, schedule and cost status, key achievements, next-period priorities, top risks/issues, resource concerns, decisions required and recovery actions. Each amber or red item needs an owner, impact, action and due date."

## 26. How do you manage business continuity during transition?

"I identify critical services, acceptable downtime, fallback procedures, support contacts and decision authority. I validate the rollback plan, test the contingency process and ensure agents know how to operate during an outage. I coordinate the cutover window with Operations and WFM and reconcile transactions captured during fallback."

## 27. What is benefits realization?

"Benefits realization is the process of confirming that the expected business outcomes actually occur after delivery. I define each benefit, baseline, target, owner, data source and measurement window before go-live. I review benefits after stabilization and distinguish implementation outputs, such as configured CRM, from outcomes, such as improved FCR or reduced response time."

## 28. What is PRINCE2 management by exception?

"It means the project board delegates authority within agreed tolerances for time, cost, scope, quality, risk and benefits. The Project Manager manages within those tolerances and escalates when a forecast breach is likely. It supports timely decisions without requiring senior leaders to manage every task."

## 29. How do you apply PMP / PMBOK in practice?

"I use the principles and performance domains to tailor delivery: value, stakeholders, team, development approach, planning, project work, delivery, measurement and uncertainty. In practice, that means clear outcomes, integrated planning, proactive stakeholder engagement, risk-based decisions and continuous measurement."

## 30. What would you do differently on your next project?

"I would validate critical assumptions and external dependencies even earlier, involve BAU owners from initiation, define KPI data sources before configuration, and test the full operational fallback process before cutover. I would also use early user feedback to identify adoption risks before the pilot."
