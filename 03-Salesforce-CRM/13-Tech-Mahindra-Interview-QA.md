# Tech Mahindra Program Manager – SPO: Interview Questions and Model Answers

> These answers follow the supplied 12-page interview guide, especially its 30 scenario questions and focus areas. Adapt examples and numbers to your actual experience. The Salesforce project below is a portfolio simulation; the ePA project should be described according to your real role and evidence.

## 1. Explain the most complex project you have managed from initiation to closure.

"I would use the Salesforce Service Cloud transformation case study to explain my end-to-end delivery approach. The scenario involves a 150-seat contact centre moving from fragmented systems to Salesforce with Avaya CTI integration. I structure the work through initiation, requirements, governance, CRM/CTI build, data migration, SIT/UAT, workforce readiness, pilot, phased rollout, hypercare and BAU. I use a WBS, integrated schedule, RAID, RACI and steering reporting to manage the work. I measure success against agreed service and adoption criteria rather than only technical completion."

## 2. How would you create a complete project plan for a new BPO client transition?

"I start by understanding the client's process, volumes, service hours, customer expectations, contractual SLAs, systems, data, locations and compliance needs. I assess feasibility, assumptions and dependencies, then define scope, deliverables, acceptance criteria and governance in the charter and SOW. I identify stakeholders and build the WBS, resource plan, budget, communications, RAID and integrated schedule. I plan recruitment, knowledge transfer, SOPs, training, infrastructure, testing, pilot, cutover, rollback, hypercare and BAU acceptance. I baseline only after the owners validate estimates and dependencies."

## 3. How do you manage multiple projects simultaneously?

"I maintain one portfolio-level view of milestones, resource demand, budget, risks, dependencies and business priority. I assess each project by strategic value, regulatory or client urgency, delivery risk and resource criticality. I use a common reporting cadence and standard RAID definitions, but retain project-specific plans. I identify shared resources early, negotiate priorities with sponsors and escalate trade-offs rather than allowing silent slippage. I also reserve capacity for critical incidents and governance decisions."

## 4. How do you identify and manage project risks?

"I identify risks through workshops, assumptions analysis, dependency reviews, lessons learned, technical assessments and operational walkthroughs. I score probability and impact, assign a named owner, define a trigger and mitigation, and record residual risk. I review high risks weekly and critical ones more frequently. For example, CTI instability could delay SIT, so I would test network and connector dependencies early, engage the vendor, define a workaround and protect the pilot date with an evidence-based recovery plan."

## 5. How would you manage a project that is significantly behind schedule?

"I first establish the facts: baseline versus actual dates, critical path, remaining effort, blocked decisions, defect trends, resource capacity and forecast finish. I separate symptoms from root causes. Then I create recovery options such as resequencing non-dependent work, adding targeted resources, reducing low-value scope through formal approval, parallelizing tasks or changing rollout waves. I compare cost, quality and operational risk, present options to the sponsor and baseline the approved recovery plan. I increase tracking frequency until the project stabilizes."

## 6. How do you handle conflicting stakeholder expectations?

"I bring the stakeholders back to the agreed business objective, contractual commitments, acceptance criteria and constraints. I document the conflicting needs, quantify their impact and facilitate a decision using options and trade-offs. If the decision changes scope, cost or timeline, I route it through change control. I record the decision, accountable approver and communication actions. I avoid making promises to one stakeholder that create hidden commitments for another team."

## 7. What would you do if a client demands an unrealistic transition timeline?

"I acknowledge the business need and assess the timeline against critical dependencies, staffing, recruitment, technology, security, training, testing and operational readiness. I prepare a feasibility assessment with options: phased scope, pilot-first rollout, parallel workstreams, additional approved resources or a revised date. I explain the risk of compressing mandatory testing or training. I recommend a plan that protects customer experience and contractual service levels, and ask the authorized sponsor to approve the trade-off."

## 8. How would you manage a project that has exceeded its approved budget?

"I validate actual spend, commitments, estimate-to-complete and the variance source. I classify the cause as scope change, estimation gap, vendor cost, resource overrun or rework. I assess the impact of corrective options, such as scope prioritization, resource optimization, vendor negotiation or phasing. I do not conceal the variance or spend beyond authority. I present the forecast and options to the sponsor, seek approval for any revised baseline and strengthen cost controls."

## 9. How would you handle a critical technology issue before go-live?

"I first assess severity, affected users, customer impact, data/security implications and available workaround. I convene the technical owner, Operations, QA and vendor support, assign a single incident lead and agree the next update time. I review whether the issue affects a go-live criterion or critical path. If the risk cannot be contained, I recommend no-go or rollback. I communicate facts and recovery options clearly and retain the incident, decision and root-cause evidence."

## 10. How do you manage project dependencies across departments?

"I make dependencies visible in the integrated schedule and dependency log, with a predecessor, successor, owner, due date and impact if missed. I validate them during workstream reviews and the daily transition scrum. For example, CTI testing depends on network/firewall readiness and Salesforce environment access. If a predecessor slips, I assess float and critical-path impact, identify work that can proceed in parallel and escalate the decision needed."

## 11. How would you handle a client escalation involving delayed project delivery?

"I respond quickly with a factual status: what has slipped, why, business impact, options, recovery owner and next update. I avoid defensive language. I validate the root cause with workstream leads, create a corrective action plan and agree dates with accountable owners. I communicate through the agreed escalation channel and track actions to closure. If a baseline change is required, I obtain formal approval and explain the implications transparently."

## 12. What would you do if Operations refused to accept the BAU handover?

"I would first understand the reason: missing SOPs, unresolved defects, unclear ownership, insufficient training, unstable KPIs or lack of support capacity. I map each gap to an acceptance criterion and owner. I agree a remediation plan and evidence required for acceptance. If service is unstable, I extend controlled hypercare through governance rather than forcing handover. I transfer ownership only when the receiving team confirms readiness and accepts the residual risks."

## 13. How do you ensure all stakeholders remain aligned throughout a project?

"I establish a stakeholder register, RACI and communication plan at initiation. I use steering meetings for decisions, operational reviews for readiness and service risks, and daily workstream coordination for blockers. I publish concise status reports with RAG, milestones, top risks, decisions required and owners. I also maintain decision and change logs so teams work from the same approved scope and assumptions."

## 14. How do you measure the success of a project or program?

"I measure delivery and business outcomes. Delivery measures include schedule adherence, budget variance, scope acceptance, defect leakage and resource utilization. Operational measures include SLA, AHT, FCR, CSAT, abandon rate, quality, productivity and uptime. I establish baselines and KPI definitions before implementation, assign data owners and review benefits after stabilization. A project is not successful merely because the technology went live; the business must adopt it and achieve the agreed outcomes."

## 15. How do you manage scope creep and last-minute change requests?

"I distinguish defects and clarifications from genuine scope changes. I log the request, confirm the business rationale, assess effort, cost, timeline, risk, security and operational impact, and present options to the Change Control Board or authorized approver. I do not allow unapproved changes into the baseline. I communicate the decision and update requirements, schedule and budget only after approval."

## 16. How would you manage a transition involving multiple locations?

"I assess each location's volumes, language needs, local processes, infrastructure, staffing, time zone, compliance and service windows. I standardize the core process but document approved local variations. I nominate site leads, maintain a common integrated plan and readiness checklist, and use a pilot site or cohort before scaling. I track location-level risks, training completion, access, testing and service performance, then sequence cutover based on readiness rather than geography alone."

## 17. What would you do if recruitment and training activities were delayed?

"I quantify the seat gap and its impact on training, pilot and service coverage. I work with HR and WFM to validate the recruitment pipeline, attendance, attrition assumptions and available cross-trained resources. I evaluate staggered cohorts, additional approved trainers, revised shifts or a phased launch. I do not reduce competency standards to protect a date. I present the revised capacity forecast and seek sponsor approval for any timeline or scope change."

## 18. How would you handle a key stakeholder who repeatedly misses commitments?

"I speak with the stakeholder directly to understand the blocker and confirm the commitment, impact and required decision. I document actions with owners and due dates, offer support to remove obstacles and agree a realistic recovery date. If the dependency remains at risk, I escalate through the governance structure with facts and options. I keep the relationship professional and focus on the project impact, not personal blame."

## 19. Explain a project failure or major setback and what you learned from it.

"I would choose a genuine example from my experience and explain the original plan, what went wrong, my responsibility, the corrective action and the lesson. A useful structure is to describe an assumption that was not validated early enough, how it affected a dependency or timeline, how I communicated it, and how I recovered. My key learning is to validate critical dependencies and decision owners during initiation, maintain realistic contingency and raise early warning signals before they become delivery crises."

## 20. How do you decide whether a project is ready for go-live?

"I use an evidence-based readiness checklist covering scope, UAT, critical defects, data integrity, security, infrastructure, licences, telephony, trained users, WFM coverage, SOPs, support model, cutover and rollback. Each criterion has an owner and evidence. I collect recommendations from Technology, Operations, QA and WFM and present a go/no-go recommendation to the authorized decision-maker. A critical unmet criterion is not waived simply because the date is near."

## 21. How do you ensure a smooth transition from project delivery to BAU?

"I plan BAU involvement from the beginning. I identify the receiving owner, support tiers, SLAs, escalation paths, knowledge articles and training needs. During hypercare, I transfer runbooks, known errors, configuration details, vendor contacts, monitoring dashboards and open actions. I confirm the support team can perform key tasks and understand incident escalation. I obtain formal operational acceptance and track residual actions after handover."

## 22. How do you communicate negative project updates to senior leadership?

"I communicate early, clearly and without hiding the issue. I state the baseline, actual position, impact, root cause, recovery options, recommendation, accountable owner and decision deadline. I avoid technical detail unless it affects a decision. I provide a revised forecast only after validating it with the delivery leads. Senior leaders need transparency and choices, not surprises."

## 23. How would you manage competing priorities across multiple projects?

"I compare priorities against business value, client commitments, regulatory risk, critical-path impact and resource availability. I make the trade-offs visible and ask sponsors to resolve conflicts that exceed my authority. I protect critical resources from unplanned work and maintain a portfolio view of milestone and capacity risk. I document priority decisions so teams understand why work has changed."

## 24. What project governance framework do you follow?

"I use a governance structure with an executive steering committee, operational review forum, workstream meetings and daily transition coordination when required. I maintain a charter, integrated schedule, WBS, RACI, RAID, decision log, change log, status report and acceptance register. Each forum has a defined purpose, decision rights, cadence and outputs. The level of governance is proportionate to project risk and complexity."

## 25. How do you apply PMP / PRINCE2 principles to your projects?

"I apply PMP practices through integrated scope, schedule, cost, quality, resource, communication, risk, procurement and stakeholder management. I use PRINCE2 concepts such as continued business justification, defined roles, management by stages, management by exception and lessons learned. I tailor the level of documentation to the project. The framework is useful when it improves control and decision-making, not when it creates paperwork without value."

## 26. How would you take ownership of a failing project and develop a recovery plan?

"I start with a rapid health assessment: scope, schedule, cost, quality, risks, resource capacity, stakeholder confidence and business impact. I validate the facts with the team and identify root causes. I establish a recovery control room, prioritize critical-path work, resolve blocked decisions, reassess scope and reset the forecast. I agree the recovery plan with sponsors, communicate it transparently and track leading indicators daily until delivery stabilizes."

## 27. What is the difference between managing a project and managing a program?

"A project delivers a defined output within an agreed scope and timeline. A program coordinates related projects and change initiatives to realize broader business benefits. Project management focuses on deliverables, constraints and acceptance. Program management also manages cross-project dependencies, strategic alignment, shared resources, benefit realization and executive-level trade-offs. For an SPO role, I would show how individual project milestones contribute to the client's operating model and strategic objectives."

## 28. How do you ensure successful client transitions without affecting service delivery?

"I protect the existing operation through feasibility assessment, WFM capacity planning, staggered training, controlled pilot, phased cutover and rollback readiness. I establish service baselines and monitor SLA, AHT, FCR, CSAT, abandon rate and staffing during transition. I do not move a cohort until access, training, technology and support are ready. I maintain a clear escalation path and a contingency process for service disruption."

## 29. How do you manage challenging stakeholders without direct reporting authority?

"I build credibility through clear commitments, reliable follow-up, data and an understanding of each stakeholder's priorities. I clarify decision rights through the RACI, make dependencies visible and explain the impact of missed commitments. I listen to concerns and offer options rather than issuing instructions. When a decision is outside my authority, I escalate through the agreed governance route."

## 30. Why are you suitable for the Program Manager, SPO position?

"I bring more than nine years of experience across project management, PMO, healthcare operations, market research and cross-functional delivery, supported by PMP and CSM certifications. I understand how to structure projects, coordinate stakeholders, manage risks, track deliverables and communicate with leadership. My healthcare ePA and Salesforce implementation case studies demonstrate my ability to connect technology, business processes, operational readiness and measurable outcomes. I am comfortable working across teams without relying on direct reporting authority, and I focus on delivery discipline and business value."

## Additional technical and operational questions

### What is the difference between CRM configuration and CRM licence provisioning?

CRM configuration sets up how the Salesforce application works: objects, fields, page layouts, roles, queues, case rules and automation. Licence provisioning obtains or assigns the appropriate user entitlement and creates/activates user access. The client usually owns licence procurement and authorization; the Salesforce administrator assigns the licence and permissions. I track this as a readiness dependency.

### What is CTI?

Computer-Telephony Integration connects a telephone system such as Avaya with Salesforce. It can support caller identification, screen-pop, softphone controls, call transfer/hold and call logging, depending on the connector and design.

### What is a screen-pop?

A screen-pop automatically displays a matching customer or case record when an incoming call arrives. Agents must still follow identity verification; caller ID alone is not proof of identity.

### What is data reconciliation?

It is the comparison of source and destination data after migration. I compare record counts, required fields, relationships and sampled values, then investigate rejected, missing or duplicate records.

### What is the difference between CTI and omnichannel routing?

CTI connects telephony to the CRM. Omnichannel routing distributes supported interactions across channels such as voice, chat, email or messaging based on configured capacity, skills and routing rules. The exact channels depend on the solution scope and licences.

### What is a 12-point CSAT improvement?

If CSAT moves from 74% to 86%, the change is 12 percentage points. The relative improvement is 16.2%. I would report the baseline, final score, survey method and sample size.

### What is AHT?

Average Handling Time is generally talk time plus hold time plus after-call work divided by handled contacts. The client's KPI definition governs what is included.

### What is FCR?

First Contact Resolution measures the share of eligible customer issues resolved during the first interaction without repeat contact for the same issue within the defined window.

### What are occupancy and shrinkage?

Occupancy measures the proportion of logged-in available time spent handling work, based on the client's definition. Shrinkage is paid time when agents are unavailable for contact handling, such as breaks, training, meetings or leave. WFM uses these assumptions to forecast staffing.

### What is a rollback plan?

A documented procedure to return to an approved prior process or system if critical cutover criteria fail. It identifies triggers, decision authority, technical steps, communication and reconciliation of in-flight work.
