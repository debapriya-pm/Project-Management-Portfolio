# Project Charter

**Project:** Salesforce Service Cloud Contact Centre Transformation  
**Project type:** Portfolio implementation simulation  
**Planning window:** 30 November 2026 – 27 February 2027 (90 calendar days)  
**Business owner:** Client VP, Operations  
**Delivery owner:** Program / Project Manager  
**Status:** Draft scenario charter – approval fields are illustrative

## 1. Business case

The client operates a 150-seat customer service process using separate ticketing, telephony and email applications. This fragmented environment limits customer context, creates duplicate effort and makes SLA performance difficult to manage consistently.

The project establishes a unified Salesforce Service Cloud operating model, integrates Avaya CTI, migrates agreed customer and case data, and transitions agents in controlled cohorts while protecting service continuity.

## 2. Objectives

1. Move 150 contact-centre agents and supervisors to a common Salesforce Service Cloud workflow.
2. Integrate Avaya telephony with Salesforce for caller identification, screen-pop and interaction logging.
3. Migrate approved customer and case records with documented reconciliation.
4. Standardize case intake, assignment, escalation, knowledge and reporting.
5. Complete pilot, phased cutover, hypercare and BAU handover within the 90-day scenario window.
6. Establish measurable operational benefits using agreed KPI definitions and baselines.

## 3. Success criteria

- All 150 agents complete training, access validation and operational readiness checks.
- Critical end-to-end scenarios pass SIT and UAT.
- No open Severity 1 defect at go-live; Severity 2 defects require documented business acceptance and workaround.
- Data migration meets approved completeness and accuracy thresholds.
- Cutover and rollback plans receive technical and operational approval.
- SLA adherence reaches at least 95% for 14 consecutive days before BAU exit.
- Uptime reaches 99.9% during the agreed measurement period.
- Client Operations and BAU owners sign the handover package.

## 4. Scope

**In scope**
- Discovery, requirements, feasibility and To-Be process design.
- Salesforce Service Cloud configuration and role-based access.
- Avaya CTI integration and call workflow validation.
- Data profiling, cleansing, mapping, migration and reconciliation.
- SIT, UAT, defect management and regression.
- SOPs, knowledge transfer, training and WFM capacity planning.
- Pilot, two rollout waves, cutover, rollback readiness, hypercare and BAU transition.

**Out of scope**
- Replacement of the Avaya telephony platform.
- New WhatsApp/SMS channels unless approved through change control.
- Unapproved changes to client products, policies or contractual SLAs.
- Data sources and historical records not included in the signed migration scope.
- Custom integrations not listed in the approved solution design.

## 6. Governance and authority

- **Executive Steering Committee:** approves material scope, budget, schedule and risk decisions; resolves escalations.
- **Operational Review Committee:** reviews readiness, staffing, service performance and open actions.
- **Daily Transition Scrum:** resolves workstream blockers and coordinates near-term delivery.
- **Project Manager:** owns integrated planning, reporting, RAID, change control, dependencies and escalation.
- **Workstream leads:** own deliverables, estimates, quality evidence and resource commitments.

## 7. Key assumptions and constraints

- Client SMEs attend discovery and UAT as scheduled.
- Salesforce and CTI environments, licences and vendor support are available when required.
- Client IT owns licence procurement and authorizes access.
- Data owners approve mapping and cleansing rules.
- Security, identity, firewall and network approvals are completed before integrated testing.
- Training is staggered so live queue service levels remain protected.
- The 90-day target is a scenario constraint and must be validated against actual technical/vendor lead times.

## 8. Initial risks

| Risk | Initial response |
|---|---|
| CTI connector or network delay | Early connectivity assessment, vendor escalation and fallback design |
| Poor legacy data quality | Early profiling, approved cleansing rules and reconciliation |
| Late licence provisioning | Named owner, dated access roster and readiness checkpoint |
| Agent resistance | Super-users, floor-walkers, coaching and feedback |
| Training affects queue coverage | WFM backfill and staggered cohorts |
| Scope expansion | Formal change request and impact assessment |
