# Executive Findings and Commercial Recommendations

## Physician efficacy perception
**Finding:** 78% of specialists rated the efficacy profile superior to current standard of care.  
**Risk:** Infection-related safety concerns were cited in a subset of responses.  
**Recommendation:** Equip field medical teams with comparative safety evidence and clear clinical education.

## Administration preference
**Finding:** 84% preferred a monthly subcutaneous autoinjector over hospital IV infusion.  
**Risk:** Patient dexterity and injection technique may affect successful home administration.  
**Recommendation:** Include an ergonomic trainer device and practical onboarding support in patient starter packs.

## Payer access
**Finding:** All 15 interviewed payers required evidence of failure on first-line generic therapy before approval.  
**Risk:** Manual prior authorization could delay treatment initiation.  
**Recommendation:** Establish a dedicated prior-authorization support concierge for prescribing clinics.

## Affordability
**Finding:** Physician intent declined when monthly out-of-pocket co-pay exceeded USD 50.  
**Risk:** Cost exposure may contribute to prescription abandonment.  
**Recommendation:** Evaluate patient affordability support options, subject to local legal, compliance and market-access review.

## Executive implication
Launch readiness depends not only on clinical confidence but also on access navigation, affordability and patient administration support.
