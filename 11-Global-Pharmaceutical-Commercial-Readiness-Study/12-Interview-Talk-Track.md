# Interview Talk Track

## 90-second opening
“I led the delivery planning for a multi-country commercial-readiness and physician-adoption research program for a newly approved biologic therapy. The scenario covered five markets—the US, UK, Germany, France and Italy—over 14 weeks, with an illustrative budget baseline of $485,000.

The research combined 60 target KOL interviews, 15 payer and hospital-pharmacy interviews, and a 250-physician online survey. My program-management focus was the integrated plan: aligning the sponsor and research teams, coordinating country fieldwork, managing compliance and safety workflows, tracking quotas and quality, and turning the findings into executive recommendations.

The main delivery challenge came in Week 7, when hospital specialist recruitment in Germany and the UK was at around 40% of the time-phased target. I led a root-cause review and introduced evening and weekend slots, bilingual senior moderators and pre-screened regional panels. In the scenario, recruitment recovered within 10 days and fieldwork remained on schedule.

The final illustrative sample was 332 respondents against a target of 325. The strategic output connected physician preference with payer step-therapy barriers and patient administration concerns, leading to recommendations for clinic-facing prior-authorization support and patient training.”

## STAR: Recruitment recovery
- **Situation:** Germany and UK hospital specialist recruitment at 40% of time-phased quota in Week 7.
- **Task:** Recover sample coverage without compromising eligibility, quality or final delivery.
- **Action:** Root-cause review; revised interview windows; bilingual moderators; specialist panels; credential pre-checks; daily funnel tracking and approved incentive contingency.
- **Result:** Scenario records a 180% increase in recruitment velocity and recovery within 10 days.

## Likely follow-up questions
**How did you control a multi-country program?**  
I used one integrated WBS and milestone plan, country-level quota trackers, a weekly sponsor review and more frequent fieldwork reviews during critical recruitment. Each workstream had a named owner, deliverable and escalation threshold.

**How did you protect research quality while accelerating recruitment?**  
I did not relax the screener. I changed access and scheduling, not eligibility criteria. Credentials were verified before interviews and quality checks continued during fieldwork.

**How did you manage adverse events?**  
I ensured moderators were trained, the sponsor's safety contact and backup route were confirmed, and a timestamped restricted log was maintained. Potential cases were routed under the approved sponsor SOP; our scenario uses a 24-hour internal target.

**What is the difference between GDPR and EphMRA / BHBIA?**  
GDPR is a legal data-protection framework. EphMRA and BHBIA provide industry research codes and guidance. They are related controls but not interchangeable.

**How did you use payer interviews?**  
As qualitative directional evidence to understand coverage logic, documentation expectations and step-therapy barriers. I would not claim 15 interviews statistically represent all payers.

**What did you present to executives?**  
A concise evidence-to-action narrative: physician enthusiasm, payer access friction, administration concerns and affordability sensitivity, followed by prioritized actions, owners and proposed launch-readiness measures.

**What would you improve next time?**  
I would pre-build reserve specialist panels, use time-of-day availability data in the recruitment forecast, and define the recruitment recovery trigger before fieldwork begins.

## Interview integrity
Use “in this case study” or “the scenario illustrates” where a metric is illustrative. Do not state that a named pharmaceutical company was the client unless you can substantiate and are authorized to disclose it. Be ready to explain which parts reflect your actual responsibilities and which are scenario design.
